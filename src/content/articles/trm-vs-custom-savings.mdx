Two engineers can analyze the same rooftop unit replacement and file completely
different numbers — both correct — because they took different routes to the claim.
Choosing the route early saves rework later.

## Deemed values

A fixed savings figure per unit installed, published by the program. Fast, cheap, and
appropriate for high-volume measures with narrow variation: LED lamps, low-flow
fixtures, basic controls. The trade is precision. If the site's operating hours are far
from the deemed assumption, the number will be visibly wrong in both directions.

## Technical Reference Manual algorithms

The middle path, and the one most industrial measures land on. The TRM specifies the
algorithm and the default inputs, and you substitute site-specific values where you
have them. It is worth reading the manual's assumptions carefully — the default
equivalent full load hours embedded in a formula often dominate the result.

## Custom analysis

Required when the measure is not in the manual, or the site's operation departs too
far from the algorithm's assumptions. This means building the calculation yourself:
bin-weather analysis, regression against metered data, or a calibrated model, plus a
Measurement and Verification plan that says how the savings will be confirmed.

## The practical rule

Use the most prescriptive route the measure and the site will support. Custom analysis
is not a mark of rigor by itself — it is the option you take when the simpler routes
would misrepresent the facility.
